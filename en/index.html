SCREENREADY GUIDE

User Guide

Version 0.2.3 日本語

ScreenReady lets you capture your screen, hide parts you don't want to show, add circles and arrows to point things out, and turn text in an image into text you can copy.

Image processing is local by default

Capture, editing, OCR, QR reading, history, and saving are processed on this PC, and images or OCR results are not sent automatically to the publisher. Web Capture, web links, and Gmail drafts connect through your browser only when you choose those actions.

Select Area and All Displays

When you open the app, there are two capture buttons in the top right.

  • Select Area — Drag (press and hold the mouse button while moving it) to capture only the part you chose. Use this when you only want a specific part of the screen.
  • All Displays — Captures the entire screen as-is. If you use multiple monitors, all of them are captured together.

Instead of the buttons, you can also use keyboard shortcuts.

Select AreaCtrl+Shift+A All DisplaysCtrl+Shift+S

Once captured, the image automatically appears in the preview in the middle of the screen.

Use the mouse wheel to zoom the preview. While zoomed in, hold Space and drag with the left mouse button to pan the image.

If you just want to capture and copy right away — use "Quick Mode" in the top right. It skips the editing screen and lets you capture and copy with one click from a small always-on-top panel, with optional auto-save to a folder you choose.
2

Markup

Use this when you want to point something out or note a mistake. Open the "Markup" tab to use it.

  • Rectangle, Circle, Arrow, LinePress the button, then drag on the image to draw.
  • TextPress the button, type your text in the box, then click where you want to place it.

Color, thickness, and text size can all be changed in the row below the buttons.

Click an item to move, resize, rotate, or delete it. Multiple selection is also supported. Save frequently used text with + Add. Undo and Redo follow operations in order across tabs and across the crop boundary.
3

Crop

Use this when you want to remove extra parts of the image. Open the "Crop" tab to use it.

  • Crop — Drag to select the part you want to keep; everything outside it is cropped away.
  • Remove Vertical Band (Join Left and Right) / Remove Horizontal Band (Join Top and Bottom) — Removes only a band-shaped part of the image and joins the rest back together. Handy when you captured a long scrolled screen but only need to remove an unwanted section in the middle.
4

Scan Text

Turns text inside a photo into text you can copy. Just opening the "Scan Text" tab does not scan it — click the badge labeled "Scan Text: Off" (it changes to "Scan Text: On") to recognize the text. Recognized text can be copied on its own or saved together with the image.

If a QR code appears in the image, it's found automatically. If its contents are a web address (URL), you need to click it a second time before it opens in your browser (this is to prevent accidentally opening it).
5

Scan Text (Mask)

Use this when you want to black out and hide something you don't want others to see, like an email address or a name. This is also part of the "Scan Text" tab.

  1. Click the badge labeled "Scan Text: Off" (it changes to "Scan Text: On").
  2. Text in the image is automatically surrounded by boxes.
  3. Click the box around text you want to hide, and that area is blacked out.
  4. You can also freely black out areas that aren't boxed by dragging over them with the mouse.
6

Web Capture

Capture an open Chrome or Edge page, including content that extends vertically beyond the visible area.

  1. Select "Detect Pages" to open the browser's remote debugging settings.
  2. Turn on Allow remote debugging for this browser instance.
  3. Return to ScreenReady and choose the target under "Page to Capture."
  4. If the browser is minimized, approve restoring it. If another tab is visible, ScreenReady switches to the selected tab.
  5. Select "Capture" and wait until the progress message closes and the image appears in the preview.
Using "Preload" may cause the target browser to fetch additional images or other resources from the website. Some site structures or loading methods can still cause capture failure, early stopping, duplication, or missing content.
7

Saving and Copying

  • To save — Check the save location at the top of the screen, then press Save. On first use, it matches the Data Save Location in Settings and can be changed later.
  • To paste somewhere (copy) — Press the copy button above the preview, or press Ctrl+C.

You can also choose whether to save or copy "image only," "text only," or "both."

8

History

The "History" tab lists everything you've captured and edited so far. Both the "original" right after capture and the "edited" version (with masks and markup added) are kept.

9

Settings

The "Settings" tab lets you do the following:

  • Data save location — Where the app stores its internal data. If you lose track of it, "Reset to Default" restores it.
  • Language — Switch between Japanese and English. Switching will prompt you to restart.
  • Background — Whether the app stays in the notification area (system tray) when you close the window. When on, you can still capture with the shortcut keys even while closed.
  • Confirmation dialogs — Whether to show a confirmation before hard-to-undo actions like "Clear All."
  • Support — Open the User Guide, report a problem or suggestion, or report an OCR result.
  • The Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, and Credits (a list of the software this app uses) can also be checked from this screen.
10

Report a Problem or Suggestion

Open a general report or OCR report from Support in Settings. After reviewing the included data and selecting consent, choose "Open Draft in Gmail." Gmail opens with the recipient, subject, and message filled in, and the report data is copied to the clipboard.

Paste it into the Gmail message, review the content and recipient, and send it yourself. ScreenReady does not sign in, attach images, or send email automatically.

11

FAQ

Q. When I switch windows, a large "あ" or "A" briefly appears on screen, and my typing switches between Japanese and half-width input on its own. Is this a ScreenReady bug?

A. No, this is not a bug in ScreenReady itself. This is a general behavior of Windows, and it can happen with other apps too — not just ScreenReady — simply by minimizing and restoring a window, or switching to another app.

Because ScreenReady's own program cannot reliably prevent this, here are the available workarounds.

  • Open Windows' language settings and turn off this notification display. (The exact setting differs depending on your OS version and environment, so please adjust it according to what's available on your system.)
  • Wait a moment for the "あ"/"A" indicator to disappear before typing.
